Title
A Structured Database and Quantitative Survey of Artificial Intelligence in Pediatric Radiology

Purpose
To construct a structured database of pediatric radiology artificial intelligence (AI) research and quantify trends, clinical coverage, and availability of reusable resources to guide research priorities.

Methods
PubMed/MEDLINE and Embase were searched for primary pediatric and fetal imaging AI research published from January 2005 through September 9, 2026, supplemented by OpenAlex records. Journal articles and preprints were eligible; reviews and conference proceedings were excluded. Automated screening and abstract-based extraction populated record-level fields for modality, age group, clinical problem, task, validation, and code/model availability. We analyzed temporal trends and overlapping modality, age, and task categories, mapped named datasets using explicit text rules, and tested modality rankings after collapsing identical normalized titles.

Results
Of 6,602 screened records, 3,496 primary-study records were included; 1,371 (39.2%) were assigned to 2025-2026. MRI accounted for 45.5%, ultrasound 23.7%, radiography 19.3%, CT 9.3%, nuclear imaging/PET 1.5%, and fluoroscopy 0.4%. Fetal imaging represented 22.5% of records. Detection/diagnosis (38.2%), measurement (24.0%), outcome prediction (19.5%), and segmentation (18.9%) predominated. Excluding an exploratory developmental-neuroscience MRI subset reduced MRI and ultrasound shares to 30.6% and 30.1%, respectively. Named dataset mentions clustered around neurodevelopmental cohorts and radiographic benchmarks. Open-source code or model availability was recorded for 168 records (4.8%); 92 (2.6%) contained a code URL. Collapsing identical titles yielded 3,414 records without changing modality rankings. Independent human validation and publication-version adjudication remain incomplete.

Conclusions
This database enables quantitative mapping of pediatric imaging AI by clinical application, age, modality, and reusable resource. Research concentrates in MRI, fetal imaging, and established diagnostic and measurement tasks, with limited documented code availability. These findings identify priorities for resource development beyond common benchmarks and age-specific clinical evaluation. Counts remain provisional because of automated extraction, unresolved duplicates, and indexing lag.
